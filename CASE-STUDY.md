# Case study: belfamuhammad.com

**Project:** my own portfolio site · **Role:** brand, copy, design, build · **Live:** https://www.belfamuhammad.com · **Stack:** Next.js, Tailwind CSS, Motion, Vercel

## The problem

I needed a site that wins remote clients, mostly small-business owners comparing freelancers on Upwork. Those visitors decide fast, often on a phone, and many read English as a second language. Two things worked against me. I had no finished client sites to show yet, and "web designer" on its own puts me in a crowd of thousands. What sets me apart is six to seven years in marketing and branding, so the site had to show that thinking at work.

## The approach

**Positioning first.** I first aimed the site at hospitality. Upwork job posts showed the demand is broader, so I changed course to "premium websites, designed by a marketer": industry-neutral, with marketing as the reason to hire me. AI comes in as an add-on inside the website work, backed by an OpenAI Academy certificate and a Columbia University certificate, rather than as a third service that would blur the message.

**A desk to work on.** I collected about twenty reference sites and noticed what I kept coming back to: work shown as physical objects you can pick up, one memorable interaction, dark and confident pages. That became the direction: a marketer's work desk. Projects sit on the desk as printed cards. Each card's back is the marketing brief behind the work: the goal, who it is for, and the action the site should get.

**Honest proof.** My demo projects are fictional businesses, so every card says "Concept · in progress" and shows the project's goal instead of an empty frame. There are no invented testimonials, logos or numbers. Prices, timeline, revisions, payment terms and ownership sit in plain view, because those are the questions a buyer asks before paying a deposit.

## Design decisions

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| Charcoal desk, card stock, one signal orange | One accent keeps the eye on the action; the paper texture makes the desk feel real |
| Archivo in condensed caps for headlines, normal width for text | One type family; hierarchy comes from size, not from a pile of fonts |
| Flip cards with the brief on the back | Shows the marketing thinking in a few seconds instead of a paragraph about it |
| Cards drop onto the desk, steps land like stamps, price tags swing | Motion that belongs to the desk world. Text never waits on an animation to appear, so the page reads even before scripts load |
| Prices and terms shown up front | Upwork buyers judge on risk; clear terms remove a reason to wait |
| Contact form as the single main action | One clear next step on every section |

## Checks before launch

- Screenshots at phone, tablet and laptop widths, reviewed and fixed
- Impeccable audit and critique; detector scan clean
- An independent finish review against the design contract; its fixes were applied until it signed off
- All copy edited with a no-AI-slop pass
- Reduced-motion mode tested: no falling cards, no smooth scrolling
- WCAG AA contrast; keyboard skip link; labelled form with clear errors
- SEO basics: sitemap, robots.txt, share image, schema.org data; Google Analytics ready

## Results

- Lighthouse on the live site: **98 / 100 / 100 / 100** on mobile and **100 / 100 / 100 / 100** on desktop (Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices, SEO)
- Live on its own domain with HTTPS, contact form delivering to email
